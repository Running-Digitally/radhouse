What I'm building toward

Always there.
Still yours.

A private, always-on agent that gets to know you, carries useful work forward and lives on infrastructure you control.

Personal agents like Grok Bot, Meta's Muse and OpenAI's Dots point toward this kind of relationship with AI. Radhouse is my open-source take on that idea, built around security, privacy and explicit boundaries.

01

A space of your own.

Private by design. Your conversations, files and memory in an environment you control, with a clear view of what stays there and what leaves.

Privacy begins with ownership.
02

Power, with clear limits.

A separate space for the agent, with limits on files, tools and network access enforced outside its prompts. Clear approval for consequential actions. Permissions you can inspect and revoke.

Security is part of the design.
03

Open to make your own.

Open-source code to inspect, run and adapt. Room to choose models and tools, understand how they connect and shape an agent around your life.

Built in the open ↗

The ambition: useful work can continue while you step away, within the access you've chosen. These are the principles guiding the build.

The build, as it stands

A private pilot.

A conversation that stays with you, files you can return to and a browser you can use together. Built around one Hermes assistant in your own space.

Pilot notes & what's next +

Ask about an attached PDF or document, then follow up. Saved originals remain available for the assistant to read the parts it needs, with source references, instead of putting the whole file into every prompt.

Library keeps your shared files close. Browser opens on demand: watch the assistant, take the controls, then return them when its reply has finished. Saved logins stay in the private browser's credential vault, with explicit save and remove controls.

This is a private pilot, with each new capability checked before activation. Scanned-document OCR, audio transcription, streamed reply text and a reliable Stop action still need qualification. Hosting Radhouse and choosing a model are separate decisions: data sent to a connected model follows that provider's handling.

Browse together.
Take the controls.

Open a browser beside your conversation, even before your first message. It's the same session your assistant uses, so you can see the page and pick up the controls when you need to.

Navigate yourself, fill a form or sign in. Return control when the assistant has finished its reply. Share a page into the conversation when you want to talk about it.

Open when you need it. Close when you are done. Hiding the view keeps the browser open; Close ends the session.
